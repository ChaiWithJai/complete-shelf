Why this shelf exists

The man in the mirror

On June 21, 2026, I published a short essay called “What is a father?” Its answer is three things — protection, guidance, unconditional love — and its last image is the man in the mirror, the one who has to provide them now. It ends with a single practice: “What is one thing I can do for myself today?”

This page is the longer answer to how that mirror produced a shelf of Great Books lectures. The question underneath it is not finished, so this page will not pretend it is: can I keep the force of my ambition without turning people into material, freezing inside anger, or abandoning the imaginative life I meant to build?

One rule governs this page. Everything on it is something I published, something you can check, or a gap I have labeled instead of filling. Where something is imagined, it says so. Where a photograph does not exist, there is no photograph.

Anger I Ambition II Tenderness III

These three words are my frame, taken from my own essays. They are not Professor Jiang’s framework, and they do not rename his lectures — his lectures keep their own questions, further down.

Chapter I

Anger

What was it protecting?

The day before the mirror essay, on June 20, I published a letter called “Dear Dad.” The facts in it are plain. My father is alive and chooses not to be present. I was “the man of the house” at three years old. I learned to mute emotion before I was ten. At 34, I am now older than my father was when he had me. The letter ends with love anyway — for my mother, for what remains, and still for him.

The first Homer lecture on this shelf carries a catalog question: what assignment does Achilles receive? That question is why the Iliad sits closest to this chapter. Achilles’ anger is immense because his wound is immense, and near the poem’s end an enemy father asks him to remember his own father — and grief, for a moment, does what force could not. I am deliberately not quoting the lecture itself: the transcripts I hold are automatic captions, and I will not publish a line I have not checked against the video.

No photograph in this chapter. There is not one to show, and I will not stage one.

I would like to write that my anger became fuel. I do not get to write that. What writing the letter did was smaller: it made the wound visible, and it let me stop passing the absence forward without examining it first. Under the anger was grief. Under the grief was a wish to be known, guided, and chosen. Naming that is the entire progress this chapter can honestly report.

Chapter II

Ambition

When did achievement become armor?

On February 4, 2026, I published the rawest thing I have written: I asked an AI to tell me what is wrong with me, and posted what came back. It called 2025 my worst year, and I agreed — while refusing to make it a pity party. It named the pattern: high-achiever, wannabe founder, thinkboi. New ideas felt like completed work. I collected frameworks instead of using them, and I used spirituality to bypass feelings I did not want to have. The line I keep from that post: the answer is usually in the work I am avoiding.

Professor Jiang enters the story here, and I want to get him right, because an earlier version of this page got him wrong. On December 23, 2025, he published an essay called “Secret History #28: Onward and Forward.” He turns 50 in 2026. It was not a letter about turning 34, and this is not a story about two men turning 34 and finding the answer.

What the essay actually describes: at 30 he was in his parents’ basement — angry, depressed, without prospects. Four years of rise followed: two successful study-abroad programs, prominence in education reform, a path toward fame and fortune. Then he walked away, because he no longer believed in being a middleman between wealthy Chinese families and Western education brands. Walking away did not save him. He was poor again, depressed again; he drank; he wandered. What finally gave his talent structure was not a better system.

“My wife has made many sacrifices to give me structure so that I may direct my talent for good.”

Xueqin Jiang, “Onward and Forward,” December 23, 2025

What I took from him is narrower than destiny. The end of his four-year rise — the threshold he walked away at — came around the age I am now, questioning a life built from visible capacity and other people’s definitions of success. His essay is evidence that choosing truth over a life that no longer feels like yours is survivable, and that another collapse can come between the choice and the structure. That is not comfort. It is a map with the hard parts left in.

I am not going to close this chapter with a framework. Collecting them is the confessed problem four paragraphs up.

Chapter III

Tenderness

Who can use the gift — and who is being used by it?

On January 19, 2026, I published a guide called “How to Learn AI on the Job (Because There’s No Other Way).” The confession inside it matters more than the advice: teaching is the love of my life. The guide exists because I broke production six times — six outages — and what survived them was not brilliance but postmortems, decision records, and workflows. I turned that into free learning material for friends, family, bartenders, baristas, the neighborhood. The goal was never tutorial performance. It was self-efficacy: a person leaving with evidence that they can do the next thing themselves.

Here is everything I will say about the park, because it is everything I can verify. My friend Alex has a fight on August 11. I train him at 6 a.m. The same park is where I go to imagine — the one place I think without a screen in front of me. No weather, no dialogue, no scene: the fight has not happened yet, and there is no photograph.

The park: Alex, a fight on August 11, 6 a.m. Those are the facts, and this frame stays empty.

Tenderness, as far as I can currently prove it, is this: another person now feels the quality of how I direct myself. If I arrive unprepared, his session gets worse. That is a smaller claim than a philosophy of care. It is also checkable, which is the point.

The shelf itself

Thirteen lectures, thirty‑nine questions

The shelf preserves 25 of Professor Jiang’s recorded sessions: 13 Great Books lectures and 12 Dante seminar sessions. Each of the 13 lectures carries three catalog questions — 39 in total, preserved exactly as written. The 12 Dante seminars carry no cataloged questions, so the shelf does not invent any for them.

Homer and the Invention of the HumanWhat assignment does Achilles receive?
The OdysseyHow can a person live after war and trauma?
Dante in ParadiseWhy must Beatrice replace Virgil as guide?

A question keeps the work open. It lets you test an old argument against a current life, which is the only use of a great book I trust. That is the entire design of this shelf: the lectures, their questions kept intact, and room for you to continue past both.

A letter

For Professor Jiang

Thank you. Not for an answer — you did not hand me one. Your December essay showed a man who walked away from a successful life because it stopped being true, collapsed again, and was finally given structure by love and responsibility rather than by a better system. I needed to see that sequence from someone who survived it.

The thank-you is the shelf itself: your thirteen lectures with their thirty-nine questions kept exactly as you wrote them, and twelve Dante sessions kept without invented metadata. A guide succeeds when the student can continue after the guide leaves. I am trying to continue.

If your travels ever bring you to New York, there is a classroom and a park I would like to show you.

For whoever found this

One small thing

If you read this far, I owe you something better than advice. Here is the one practice I actually use, from the mirror essay, quoted exactly:

“What is one thing I can do for myself today?”

Answer it once, small, today. That is the whole assignment.

I checked this story against the oldest template — separation, initiation, return. Separation I can document. Initiation is ongoing. Return is unproven. So this page ends without an arrival: the fight is on August 11, the questions are open, and the man in the mirror is still being answered.
